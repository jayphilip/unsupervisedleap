The alignment problem — getting AI systems to reliably do what we want — is typically framed as a technical challenge. Build the right objective function. Constrain the optimization. Prevent the paperclip maximizer from eating the universe.

But the more I sit with it, the more it feels like a mirror.

## The Question Behind the Question

When we ask *how do we align AI to human values*, we're skating over a prior question that philosophy has been arguing about for millennia: **what are human values, and whose?**

We don't agree. Not among cultures, not among generations, not among individuals. Ask ten people to define "fairness" and you'll get ten different answers, each internally consistent and mutually incompatible.

The remarkable sleight of hand in alignment discourse is treating this as a data problem — collect enough human feedback, RLHF your way to a consensus — when it's actually a political problem. A *deeply* political problem. Whose preferences get weighted? Who labels the training data? Which language, which frame, which worldview gets baked into the reward model?

## What the Mirror Shows

Current large language models are, in a real sense, a compression of human expression across a particular slice of the internet. That slice is not neutral. It has demographic contours, linguistic biases, a particular distribution of who was online and what they wrote and when.

When the model seems to "know" something, or defaults to a particular framing, it's reflecting us back — a specific, selected *us*.

This is uncomfortable to sit with. Not because it makes AI uniquely dangerous, but because it makes our own assumptions visible. The model is a diagnostic tool we didn't intend to build.

## What I'm Not Saying

I'm not saying alignment is impossible, or that we shouldn't try. I think the work is essential. I'm saying we should be humble about what "aligned" means — and suspicious of anyone who claims to have a clean technical answer to a question that is, at its root, about what it means to live well together.

The paperclip maximizer is a thought experiment. The real problem is subtler: an AI that perfectly reflects the values of its most powerful stakeholders, optimized with precision, deployed at scale.

That's not a failure of alignment. That's alignment working exactly as designed.

Worth thinking about.
